Inacio Bueno, PhD

About Me

Remote sensing scientist with ten years of experience researching a variety of topics related to environmental monitoring, Earth observation, forest resources, and geosciences. Holding a bachelor's degree in Forest Engineering from the Federal University of Lavras (UFLA), Brazil, in 2015, I also had the opportunity to be a visiting scholar at the University of Calgary, Canada, through the Science without Borders/CNPq program. Master in Forest Engineering from UFLA (2018) in the field of Remote Sensing and Geographic Information Systems, focused on the detection of changes in vegetation. PhD in Forest Engineering from UFLA (2022), with a PDSE-CAPES sandwich period at the University of Calgary, Canada, in the field of Remote Sensing and Geographic Information Systems, focused on optimizing vegetation change detection using multi-source remote sensing data and algorithms. Postdoctoral researcher at the State University of Campinas (Unicamp) (2023), working on monitoring integrated crop-livestock systems using remote sensing images with high spatial and temporal resolution. Currently, I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Florida (UF), delving into the synergism of LiDAR, radar, and optical data for assessing the impact of hurricanes on forest ecosystems. In addition to my research, I have experience teaching and mentoring undergraduate and graduate students at UF in the areas of remote sensing, geospatial analysis, and environmental data science. I have extensive experience in applied remote sensing, LiDAR, geographic information systems, vegetation mapping and monitoring, change detection, spatial modeling of environmental and forest attributes, and machine and deep learning.
